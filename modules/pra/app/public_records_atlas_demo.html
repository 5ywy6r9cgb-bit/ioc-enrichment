How to file a public-records request (Ohio)

Ohio's Public Records Act (O.R.C. § 149.43) gives any person the right to inspect and copy public records. You do not have to say who you are or why you want the records.

1. Find the right office

Use the map to identify which office likely holds the record. Click a point to see its role, the records it may hold, and its public-records contact.

2. Be specific

Describe the records as precisely as you can — the agency, the type of record, and a date range. Specific requests get faster, cleaner responses.

3. Ask for aggregate or redacted data when possible

If you only need totals or policy-level information, say so. Ask the office to redact any exempt personal information and provide the rest.

4. Keep it on record

Email or the office's online portal creates a paper trail. Note the date you sent it and any tracking number you get back.

5. Follow up

Response time depends on the request's size and the office's backlog. A polite follow-up after a reasonable wait is normal and appropriate.

This panel is general civic education, not legal advice.
