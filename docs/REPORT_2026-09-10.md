**The spread is 5.3x and it does not sort by party.** The top of the list is
a Democrat and so is a member near the bottom; the same is true of the
Republicans. That is what makes this a measurement rather than a talking
point — a table that sorted by party would be an argument with numbers
attached.

**What a high PAC share is NOT.** It is not corruption, not a scandal, and
not unusual. Members with safe seats and committee assignments attract
institutional money; members who raise nationally from individuals do not.
The figure describes where the money came from. It says nothing about what
anyone did for it.

**Script note:** the honest framing is *"two in five dollars"*, not *"nearly
half"*. 39.5% is 39.5%.

## 1.3 Legislation: two bills, named under oath by two parties each

```bash
bin/sentinel doc bills
```

Of 64 lobbying documents fetched, **three name a bill at all**. Sixty-one
are cover pages carrying income and expense boxes and no issue detail.

| bill | |
|---|---|
| **H.R. 4024 / S. 2291** | Energy Infrastructure Act |
| — same filings — | Zero-Emission Nuclear Power Production Credit Act |

**Two clients told Congress under 2 U.S.C. §1603–1604 that they lobbied on
that bill.** That is two sworn statements about one object.

**It does NOT establish that they took the same side.** A filing names the
bill; it does not say for or against. Opposing parties appear on the same
bill in the same quarter as a matter of routine, and reporting a shared bill
as an alignment is the easiest way to publish something false out of
accurate records.

**Why only three of 64:** bill numbers live on the *issue-activity* pages,
and the corpus is mostly cover sheets. **The lobbying-to-legislation link is
not yet built.** Anyone saying otherwise from this data is reading 61 blank
pages as agreement.

## 2.1 The surveillance vendors, and what the registers actually show

**A correction first, because it governs everything in this part.**

The first sweep of 19 surveillance vendors returned ~1,600 leads. **Roughly
half were an artifact of a query built wrong** — the subject was sent to
three full-text APIs unquoted, so they matched the words separately:

| searched | returned | because |
|---|---|---|
| **Axon** Enterprise | Tox Profile for Carbon Disulfide | **axon** is anatomical; carbon disulfide is a classic **axonal** neurotoxin |
| **Flock** Safety | migratory bird hunting regulations | **flock** is ornithological |
| **Peregrine** Technologies | Fish & Wildlife notices | a falcon |
| **Fusus** | 1990s Colombian cut-flower dumping | a taxonomic term |

Fixed. **Both sweeps must be re-run before anything from CourtListener, the
Federal Register or Regulations.gov under those subjects is used.**

**What was never affected, and why it matters:** Senate LDA filters
structured `client_name` fields. USAspending posts a structured recipient
filter. OpenCorporates queries a company index. **Everything below survives
because a keyword collision cannot produce "Holland & Knight, $80,000, Axon
Enterprise Inc."**

**FLOCK SAFETY RETURNED NOTHING FROM USASPENDING.**

## 3.2 A lobbying fee is not political money

```bash
bin/sentinel connect fecpac "Mercury Public Affairs"
→ No committee of that name is registered with the FEC.
```

Mercury Public Affairs bills Flock Safety **$120,000** and BGR bills
**$90,000** — real, specific, falsifiable numbers from sworn filings.

**Mercury runs no federal political committee.** So the lobbying relationship
does not connect to the money side, at least not through a PAC of its own.

**Two things that must not be conflated:** a firm's own PAC is its
employees' money. Its client's PAC is the client's. Flock Safety returned
nothing from the FEC either — consistent with a company that sells locally
and may simply run no federal committee.

# PART FOUR — THE NUMBERS, AND WHOSE THEY ARE

## 4.1 The correction that governs this section

**A published figure in the working file was wrong by a factor of 18 to 30,
and the reason is the subject of this part.**

| | |
|---|---:|
| ~~OUTSIDE PRESSURE on Husted~~ | ~~40.19x~~ |
| divided $19.1m by | $475,873 — **a truncated sample of rows** |
| his committee actually reported | **$14,350,624** |
| **corrected** | **1.35x** on receipts, **2.24x** excluding transfers |

**The truncation was reported honestly at every step. The figure was used
anyway.** It carried the words *ceiling* and *the error runs the flattering
way*, and that was not enough — **a ceiling nobody tests is a wrong number
with a disclaimer on it.**

The fix was not more caution in the labelling. It was reading a different
endpoint: one that reports the committee's own total instead of counting
rows until it hits a cap.

**1.35x is still a fact and a duller one.** Outside groups moved about a
third more than the campaign raised. **A contested seat, not an overwhelmed
one.**

### TEAM HUSTED split $8,253,532 five ways

| participant | received | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| **HUSTED FOR SENATE** | $5,215,998 | **63.2%** |
| NRSC | $1,129,393 | 13.7% |
| JOBS OPPORTUNITY NOW PAC | $824,033 | 10.0% |
| **OHIO FLYER PAC** | $604,861 | **7.3%** |
| Ohio Republican Party | $479,247 | 5.8% |

**The five account for the total exactly.**

**So an independent-expenditure-only committee is a participant in a joint
fundraising committee whose largest participant is the candidate's own
campaign.**

**This is where the report stops, deliberately.** A super PAC may not legally
coordinate with the candidate it supports. Whether — and under what
conditions — such a committee may participate in a candidate's joint
fundraiser **is a question of FEC rules and advisory opinions.** There is
guidance; it has been litigated; **these filings do not answer it and
neither does this desk.**

**NOTHING HERE ASSERTS A VIOLATION.** Three documents would move it forward:
the Form 1, the joint fundraising agreement, and the advisory opinions read
directly.

**And it is singular.** Ohio Flyer PAC does **not** appear in either of the
other two joint fundraisers. It is one arrangement, not a pattern.

## 4.8 The ratio trap, stated once

| seat | ratio | money |
|---|---:|---:|
| Wasserman Schultz 2024 | **250 : 1** | $98,905 |
| Sherrod Brown 2026 | **10 : 1** | $13,471,230 |

# PART FIVE — WHAT THIS DESK GOT WRONG

**Five corrections in one working day. They belong in the script because the
method is the story.**

| what was published | what was true |
|---|---|
| Husted OUTSIDE PRESSURE **40.19x** | **1.35x** — divided by a truncated sample |
| Three committees "converging" on two seats | Two of them were on **opposite sides of all 8** candidates they shared |
| AIPAC forwarded **$46,350** in 2022 | **$89,610** — the first came from a truncated conduit-side pull |
| Half a surveillance sweep called "noise to filter" | **The query was built wrong.** Unquoted phrases |
| `doc bills` reported **S. 2024** and **S. 82** | Neither exists. Matched inside "**U.S.** 2024" |

**Every one was caught the same way: by dividing, or by reading the same
figure from the other side.**

- The AIPAC error surfaced when the recipient's filings disagreed with the conduit's.
- The 40.19x surfaced when the delegation view disagreed with the member view.
- The convergence died when the side split was read.
- A `$121,461` gap surfaced because two Sherrod Browns were being added together.

**That is the spine of the episode if it needs one:** *we killed our own best
finding, twice, and the tool now refuses to produce the number that fooled
us.*
