## Purpose

To ensure every TSR investigation is tested against multiple expert
perspectives before any conclusion is drawn or published. Dead ends are
documented as dead ends. Expert consensus is compared against findings. All
angles are pursued. Nothing is published until it is sourced.

This methodology separates The Sentinel Report from opinion and elevates it to
documented accountability journalism.

---

## Section one — what the Glass Mark technique is

For every documented finding, three distinct expert perspectives are pulled and
compared against the evidence. The goal is not to confirm what we already think
— it is to test it, pressure it, and either strengthen it or identify where it
needs more documentation.

### The three lenses

**Lens 1 — the domain expert.**
What does the leading researcher in this specific field say about what we're
seeing? If we're looking at surveillance contracts, what do privacy law
scholars and civil liberties researchers document about these specific contract
terms? If we're looking at energy committee conflicts, what do utility
regulation academics say about documented patterns like this nationally? Domain
experts give us the established knowledge baseline.

**Lens 2 — the dissenting view.**
What would the strongest credible counterargument say? What legitimate
explanation exists for what we're seeing that isn't the one we're pursuing? A
good investigator steelmans the opposition before publishing. **If we can't
answer the dissent with documented evidence, we're not ready to publish.**

**Lens 3 — the adjacent field.**
What does someone from a completely different discipline see when they look at
this same evidence? A sociologist looking at a surveillance contract sees
different things than a lawyer. A public health researcher looking at water
contamination data sees different things than an environmental attorney.
Adjacent perspectives surface questions the primary field has normalized or
missed.

## Section two — how to apply it, step by step

### Step 1 — start with the document

The triangulation always begins with a primary source document. A signed
contract. A court filing. A financial disclosure. A government press release.
This is the anchor. Without a documented anchor, triangulation has nothing to
test against.

### Step 2 — state your hypothesis clearly

Before researching expert opinion, write down in one sentence what the document
appears to show. Be specific. Not *"this looks corrupt"* but *"this contract
grants a perpetual data license that survives contract termination, which may
conflict with the city's stated data protection policy."*

Specific hypotheses get specific expert responses. Vague hypotheses get vague
answers.

### Step 3 — pull the three expert perspectives

- Search for peer-reviewed research on the specific issue — Google Scholar,
  SSRN, JSTOR
- Find the top civil society organizations working on this issue — ACLU, EFF,
  Brennan Center, Brookings, Urban Institute
- Find at least one credentialed academic whose published work covers this
  exact territory
- Find the strongest published counterargument from a credible source — not a
  partisan outlet
- Find one adjacent field perspective — public health, economics, sociology,
  constitutional law

### Step 4 — compare and document

Place your hypothesis next to each expert perspective. Mark where they align,
where they diverge, and where they raise new questions. This comparison becomes
the "Research Triangulation" section of every TSR investigation document.

### Step 5 — classify the result

| result | what it means | what you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Amber** | Expert perspectives raise new questions or reveal documentation gaps | The investigation continues. Do not publish the claim yet. |
| **Green** | Expert perspectives corroborate the documented finding from multiple angles | The claim is supported and can be published with citations. |
| **Dead end** | Expert perspectives provide a well-documented legitimate explanation for what the document shows | Document the dead end, close the thread, move to the next question. **A documented dead end is still honest journalism.** |
