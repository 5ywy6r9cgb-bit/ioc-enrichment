## 3. AIPAC's $2.09m is 97% other people's money

**GREEN.** `bin/sentinel connect fecpac`, FEC Schedule B, cycle 2022.

AIPAC PAC (C00797670) disbursed **$2,089,843 across 2,482 rows**. Three
figures in the same filing say that is not the organisation's giving:

- **$248,936 to one campaign committee.** A PAC may give $5,000 per candidate
  per election. That row is fifty times the cap.
- **Average payment $842** — the size of a person's donation, not a
  committee's.
- **2,418 of 2,482 rows carry a memo code** — 97.4%.

Those are the signature of a **conduit**: individuals earmark contributions
for a named candidate and the committee forwards them. The money is theirs,
disclosed under their own names, moving through.

Reported as "AIPAC gave $2 million to candidates" it is false, and it is the
most common error made with this exact filing. The committee's own giving is
the residue — the ~64 rows without a memo code.

**Found by arithmetic failing a fact about the world.** The number was
internally consistent, correctly formatted and traceable to a real filing.
What caught it was a $5,000 statutory cap that $248,936 cannot satisfy.

## 6. FARA is the wrong register, and the absence is the finding

**GREEN.** FARA covers agents of a **foreign principal**. A domestic advocacy
organisation registers under the LDA and appears nowhere in FARA, so a FARA
search returns nothing — and that nothing is a fact about the register, not a
failed search.

Whether a given organisation *ought* to be a FARA registrant is a live public
argument that no database settles. Report the absence as an absence.

AIPAC's LDA footprint is complete and small: **100 filings, one client, and
the client is the registrant.** In-house lobbying, no outside firm retained
under that name. That does not rule out outside firms filing with AIPAC as
*client* — a different query.

## 14. TEAM HUSTED is a joint fundraising committee, and that changes the question

**Why the designation matters more than the amount.** A JFC is not a donor
in the ordinary sense. It is a shared vehicle: two or more committees raise
money together and split the proceeds by a written formula agreed in
advance, and the participants are named on its Form 1. Money leaving a JFC
goes to its **participants**.

So the $513,230 is not obviously a gift from one committee to another. **It
is more likely a distribution**, which would mean Ohio Flyer PAC is a
participant in a joint fundraising committee named for the candidate it
spent $1,117,199 supporting.

**That is a materially different structure from a donation, and it is NOT
established here.** What is established is the designation and the amount.
The participant list is a filing:

```bash
bin/sentinel connect fecpac "Team Husted" --committee C00896928 --cycle 2026
bin/sentinel connect donors "Team Husted" --committee C00896928 --cycle 2026
```

The first shows every recipient — the other participants appear as the other
rows. The second shows who put money in. **Neither has been run.** Until
they have, the only defensible sentence is: *a joint fundraising committee
called TEAM HUSTED, registered 2025-01-30, transferred $513,230 to Ohio
Flyer PAC.*

### Why a shared vendor is different from a shared bill

**A shared bill is routine.** Opponents file on the same bill in the same
quarter constantly; §1.3 of the report explains why that is close to
meaningless.

**A shared vendor is a contract.** Two committees paying the same firm have
each entered a commercial relationship with it. That is a structural fact
about how the operation is staffed, and it does not happen by coincidence
the way a bill number does.

### AND IT IS NOT EVIDENCE OF COORDINATION

**This must be said as loudly as the finding.**

Common vendors between a super PAC and a candidate's committees are
**ordinary**, extremely widespread, and **directly addressed by FEC rules** —
including a **firewall safe harbour at 11 CFR 109.21(h)**, under which a
vendor serving both sides implements a written firewall separating the
staff who work for each.

**Nothing here establishes that any firewall was absent, breached, or even
required.** What is established is that the same firms were paid by both.

**What would move it forward:** whether these firms have a firewall policy on
file, and whether the same individuals worked both sides. Neither is in any
API. Both are documents or interviews.

## 25. The pipeline manufactures citations. Traced end to end, reproduced.

**RED. Nothing from this pipeline may be published until this is fixed.**
This is not a style note. The visual pipeline is currently generating, in
its own output, statements that name sources which do not exist.

### The artefact that shows it

`skeptic_review.docx` — the Skeptic Agent's review of the Ohio script:

> **clm_001:** The 2026 Ohio Senate special election is projected to be the
> most expensive Senate race in American history.
> **What supports this: Supported by Congressional records. The records
> directly state this.**

**No congressional record states this.** It is a projection by AdImpact, a
private advertising-tracking firm. The claim's own wording says *projected*.
The review asserts a documentary source for a forecast — and it is the
video's HOOK.

Same page, clm_005:

> As of July 2026, nearly $155 million had already been spent **according to
> AdImpact**.
> **What supports this: Supported by Congressional records.**

**The claim names its source in its own text, and the reviewer overrides it
with a different one.**

### Where "Congressional records" comes from

`visual_director_v3.py`, `ClaimExtractor._infer_source`:

```python
if "senate" in sentence_lower or "congress" in sentence_lower:
    return "Congressional records"
```

**The string is produced by the word "Senate" appearing in the sentence.**
An Ohio *Senate* race, the *Senate* Leadership Fund — any of them returns
"Congressional records", and the Skeptic Agent prints it as a source.

Reproduced against the four real claims:

| claim contains | inferred source |
|---|---|
| "Ohio **Senate** special election… projected" | Congressional records |
| "…spent on the Ohio **Senate** race, according to AdImpact" | Congressional records |
| "**Senate** Leadership Fund… announced plans" | Congressional records |
| "Ohio alone accounts for the largest increase" | Primary filing |

**None of those four has been read from any register by this system.**

### And the evidence state that licenses it

Same class, one step earlier — `_classify_state`:

```python
if not scores:
    return "DOCUMENTED"  # Default
```

**An unclassifiable sentence is returned as DOCUMENTED — the strongest
state in the vocabulary.** Two of the four claims above matched no pattern
at all and were labelled DOCUMENTED by that default, including the
"most expensive in American history" superlative.

The patterns that *do* match are matching **the shape of evidence, not its
presence**: `r"\$\s*[\d,.]+"` marks any sentence containing a dollar sign as
DOCUMENTED. It is a prose classifier wearing an evidence classifier's name.
**That is the Flock lesson again** — the tool reported a cause it had not
checked, and the remedy it implied was the wrong one.

### The chain, in full

| step | file | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `_classify_state` | unmatched sentence → **DOCUMENTED** |
| 2 | `_infer_source` | the word "Senate" → **"Congressional records"** |
| 3 | Skeptic Agent | prints **"Supported by Congressional records"** |
| 4 | shorts generator | prefixes **"The filing indicates…"** |
| 5 | Sources Package | every entry: **"Provenance: unverified"** |

**Step 5 is the system telling the truth and steps 1-4 are the system
contradicting it.** The sources package is right: nothing has been verified.

### The review is boilerplate, not skepticism

Across all nine claims, three fields are **byte-identical**:

- *"No direct contradiction identified in the text. Check primary sources."*
- *"A critic would argue this conflates legal activity with impropriety."*
- *"Risk: low"*

The critic line is applied to clm_003 — *"the previous record for a midterm
cycle was $8.9 billion nationally in 2022"* — a claim that alleges no
impropriety by anyone. **A skeptic that says the same thing about every
claim has not read any of them**, and its "Risk: low" on an unverified
superlative is worse than no risk rating at all.

### What it does NOT establish

**Nothing here says the claims are false.** The $749m projection and the
$155m spent may well be exactly right, and AdImpact may be a perfectly good
source for them. **The defect is the provenance, not the arithmetic** — the
system is attributing real claims to sources it never read.

### The fix, in the order it must happen

1. **`_classify_state` must default to `UNKNOWN`, not `DOCUMENTED`.**
   Absence of a matched pattern is absence of evidence.
2. **`_infer_source` must be deleted or renamed.** It does not infer a
   source; it guesses a register from a keyword. If it stays, it must return
   `source_hint` and nothing downstream may print it as "Supported by".
3. **A claim's evidence state must come from a capture, not from its
   prose.** The connector side already produces exactly what is needed —
   `evidence/captures/*.json` with a sha256 in `provenance.jsonl`. **A claim
   is DOCUMENTED when a capture hash backs it and UNKNOWN when one does
   not.** Nothing else.
4. **The Skeptic Agent must be able to return "I have nothing to say about
   this claim"** rather than filling every field.

### The one that connects to the render

`EditorialSafetyChecker` already requires `source_url` **and** `sha256`
before an asset may be EVIDENCE. That rule is correct and it is the model
for claims. **The asset side of this system already refuses to call
something evidence without a hash. The claim side does not.**

## 34. The strongest numbers had the weakest audit trail

**Confirmed by reading the code, not by assuming it.**

`R.runConnector` — the path every ordinary connector takes — writes the raw
bytes to a capture, hashes them **before anything is derived**, and appends
a GREEN record to `provenance.jsonl`.

**The FEC commands do not take that path.** `fecpac`, `vendor`, `donors`,
`asymmetry`, `control`, `filings`, `member` and `delegation` all drive
`fecFetcher`, which did this:

```js
if (res.status === 200) return JSON.parse(res.body);
```

**Parsed, returned, bytes discarded.**

So the position was exactly inverted from what it should be:

| finding | capture | sha256 | ledger |
|---|---|---|---|
| a speculative OpenCorporates name search | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| **QUINTUS, LLC — $704,890, five committees, §26** | **✗** | **✗** | **✗** |
| **SRCP MEDIA — $31,000,126 against two candidates, §31** | **✗** | **✗** | **✗** |

**Every figure an editor is most likely to challenge was the one with
nothing behind it.** A number copied out of a terminal is not reproducible
six months later; a number with bytes and a hash is.

Fixed. `fecFetcher` now takes a capture spec and every page is written,
hashed and recorded. **Pages are never merged** — merged bytes are bytes no
server ever sent.

Captures are prefixed `fec_api_` rather than `live_capture_fec_`, because
`fec` is the candidate-search connector and `crosslink` would otherwise
parse Schedule B rows with the wrong parser and put them in the entity
index.

### And a second thing, found while checking the first

**The ledger was recording the FEC API key.**

The FEC takes its key as a **query parameter**, and `makeRecord` stored
`source_url` verbatim:

```
"source_url": "https://api.open.fec.gov/v1/candidates/search/?api_key=<the real key>&q=..."
```

**`provenance.jsonl` is the file you would hand to a lawyer, an editor or a
collaborator to show where a number came from.** It is the artefact most
likely to be passed to another person by hand, and it had a live credential
in it.

**Not as bad as it could have been:** `/evidence/*` and `manifests/` are
both in `.gitignore`, so this never reached the remote. It was a secret
sitting in a local file whose entire purpose is to be shown to other people.

`redactUrl` now replaces the value of `api_key`, `key`, `token`,
`access_token`, `secret`, `password` and `signature` with `REDACTED` before
the record is written — on the new FEC path **and on the existing connector
path, which is where it was actually landing.** The parameter NAME survives,
because *which* credential was used is occasionally a fair question and what
it was never is.

**Existing ledger entries still contain the key.** They are local and
gitignored. If that ledger is ever going to be shown to anyone, rotate the
FEC key first — it is free and takes a minute at api.data.gov.

## 35. Anomalies ranked by rarity, not by size

**`anomalies.csv`, alongside `pattern_map.csv`. They answer different
questions and both are worth having.**

| file | ranks by | same for every library? |
|---|---|---|
| `pattern_map.csv` | **SHAPE** — the AEP split first, then cross-register presence, then shared clients | yes |
| `anomalies.csv` | **RARITY within YOUR library** | no — it moves as the library grows |

**Two firms sharing a client that forty other firms also carry is
arithmetic. Two firms sharing a client only they carry is a relationship.**
Nothing here scores on magnitude:

- a shared client is weighted **1 / (how many firms carry it)**
- a shared firm is weighted **1 / (how many clients it has)**
- overlap is expressed as a share of the **smaller** firm's book, so two
  boutiques sharing everything outranks two giants sharing three

Verified as a property, not an intention: a pair sharing **one** client
nobody else carries outranks a pair sharing **three** clients that ten firms
each carry. And the reverse case is pinned too — twenty *exclusive* shared
clients beats one, because rarity accumulates and that is the intended
behaviour.

### The score is a sort order and the file says so

**A score of 8.4 means nothing on its own and must never appear in a
script.** It means "look at this before the one scoring 2.1". Every row
carries its components — `rarity`, `overlap_of_smaller_book`,
`shared_count`, `a_clients`, `b_clients` — so the reader can see **which
term drove it** rather than trusting the total.

And the header states the population it was measured against:

> *Rarity is measured against what you have ASKED, not against lobbying.
> This library holds N registrant(s) and M client(s); a narrow library ranks
> ordinary relationships as rare.*

**That caveat is the §32 lesson applied before the fact rather than after
it.** A confident label over a thin denominator is how a normal relationship
becomes a finding.

## 36. A dead router produced 150 findings that were not findings

**The `expand --limit 181` run died when the connection dropped. What it
printed afterwards is the problem.**

```
  fail  AXON ENTERPRISE — ENOTFOUND
  fail  PALANTIR TECHNOLOGIES, INC. — ENOTFOUND
  fail  FIRSTENERGY CORP — ENOTFOUND
  fail  MERCURY PUBLIC AFFAIRS, LLC — ENOTFOUND
  … 150 more
```

**ENOTFOUND is DNS. ETIMEDOUT is a connection that never opened.** Neither
is a fact about the organisation named beside it — but a hundred and fifty
lines of `fail <FIRM>` reads exactly like *"these firms have no filings"*,
and it was produced at roughly one per second by a tool that had no idea the
network was gone.

**This is the same failure as every other one in this file**: the tool
stated with confidence something it had not measured. It measured a socket
and reported it as a register.

### Three fixes

**1. A circuit breaker.** Three transport failures in a row stops the run.
Not "fail" — `down`, with a counter, and the words *"network, not an
answer"* beside it. A 404 or a 429 does **not** trip it, because those are
answers: a 429 means *later*, not *broken*.

**2. The abort says what it does not know.**

```
  STOPPED — the network went away.
    asked      31 of 181 before it dropped
    not asked  147  (nothing was recorded about these)
```

**"Not asked" and "asked, got nothing" are different facts** and the old
output merged them into one word.

**3. It resumes, with no new state file.** `runConnector` already writes
`search_mode` and `subject` onto every ledger record, so **the record of
what was asked is the same record that proves what was fetched.** `expand`
now reads the ledger, skips registrants already asked, and prints the count:

```
  already asked            31  skipped — --again to re-ask
```

`--again` forces the whole list, which is what you want when the library is
weeks old and page 1 has moved on.

### What to do now

**Nothing was lost.** Every page that succeeded before the drop is captured,
hashed and in the ledger. Re-running the same command picks up where it
stopped:

```bash
bin/sentinel connect expand --limit 181
```

### A note on the successful rows

Every firm that answered reported **`0 new client(s) on page 1`** —
including VENABLE LLP at 8,808 filings and MEHLMAN CONSULTING at 6,983.

**That is plausible and it is not proof of anything.** Page 1 is the 25 most
recent filings, and a large practice's most recent 25 are dominated by its
biggest retainers, which this library already holds. **It is also exactly
what a broken client-name comparison would look like**, so it is worth one
check rather than an assumption:

```bash
bin/sentinel connect senatelda --registrant "VENABLE LLP" --pages 20
```

**If twenty pages also yield nothing new, the comparison is working and the
library is genuinely broad. If they yield dozens, `expand` is only ever
going to see the surface** — and the useful command was always the deep one.

## 42. The cut-out list is not a finding, and here is the proof from your own terminal

**The 139 `fail … ENOTFOUND` lines were read as a pattern about the firms.
They are a pattern about one afternoon's connection, and two facts settle
it.**

### 1. Every one of those requests goes to the SAME HOST

`registry.js`, the senatelda connector:

```
https://lda.gov/api/v1/filings/
```

**One hostname, for all 181 requests.** ENOTFOUND is a DNS resolution
failure — the machine could not turn `lda.gov` into an address.

**DNS cannot select by which firm's name is in the query string.** The
resolver never sees "PALANTIR TECHNOLOGIES"; it sees `lda.gov` and fails or
succeeds for all of them equally. There is no mechanism by which the LDA, or
anyone else, could block Palantir's row and serve Skydio's — the query
string is not visible to DNS at all.

### 2. The same firms SUCCEEDED in the adjacent run

From the earlier terminal, same session, same machine:

| firm | in the "cut out" list | in the successful run |
|---|---|---|
| HOLLAND & KNIGHT LLP | ENOTFOUND | **ok — 20,071 filings · 22 new clients** |
| SIDLEY AUSTIN LLP | ENOTFOUND | **ok — 2,969 filings · 19 new clients** |
| PALANTIR TECHNOLOGIES, INC. | ENOTFOUND | **ok — 64 filings** |
| AXON ENTERPRISE | ENOTFOUND | **ok — 18 filings** |
| MICROSOFT CORPORATION | ENOTFOUND | **ok — 101 filings** |
| MERCURY PUBLIC AFFAIRS, LLC | ENOTFOUND | **ok — 5,987 filings · 1 new client** |
| FIRSTENERGY CORP | ENOTFOUND | **ok — 110 filings** |
| CELLEBRITE INC. | ENOTFOUND | **ok — 19 filings** |

**A block that can be defeated by running the same command twenty minutes
later is a router, not a gatekeeper.**

### You already have the data

The successful run captured **905 new client names** and the ledger now
holds **180 of 181 registrants**. That is why the resume run reported
"already asked 180, skipped" — the work is done and on disk, hashed.

### The instinct was right; the register was the wrong place to apply it

**Noticing where something stops is exactly the right reflex**, and it is
the reflex that found Quintus. It has to be aimed at a register that can
answer, and a DNS error is not one. The general rule:

> **Before treating an absence as meaningful, ask what would have had to be

does not:

```bash

```

**A failed call writes no ledger record**, so nothing in that report is
inferred from a log — a name is captured or it is not. Use it instead of
reading a failure list.

**Whether the refusal is about this NAME or about that MINUTE.** Both

## 46. SOLVED: three letters of a surname

**The ladder resolved it in four requests.**

| query | contains | result |
|---|---|---|
| `MCGINLEY LLC` (client parameter) | **cgi** | **403** |
| `MCGIN` | **cgi** | **403** |
| `GINLEY` | — | **200** — 0 filings |
| `MCNEES WALLACE & NURICK` | — | **200** — 25 filings |

**M-C-`G`-`I`-N.** The refused strings contain **`cgi`**. The accepted ones
do not.

And from the full run, the control that kills the obvious alternative:
**MCGUIREWOODS CONSULTING** and **MCALLISTER & QUINN** both answered
normally. They share the `MC` and MCGUIREWOODS shares the whole `MCG` —
**so it is not a prefix rule. It is those three letters, in that order.**

### What `cgi` is

**Common Gateway Interface.** Generic web-application-firewall rules refuse
request strings containing it, because of decades-old `/cgi-bin/` exploits —
Shellshock among them.

**Nothing is denying anything about McGinley LLC.** A rule written for a
1990s attack is eating three letters of a surname.

### This is the more useful finding: a systematic blind spot

**Any organisation whose name contains those letters is unreachable through
this route.** That is not one firm — it includes **CGI FEDERAL**, a large
federal IT contractor, which is exactly the kind of name this desk would
search.

**An empty result for a name like that would read as "this company does not
lobby."** That is §42's failure with a different cause: a fact about the
infrastructure, presented as a fact about the subject.

`firewallSnag` now names it before the operator builds a theory, on both the
registrant and the single-connector paths, and says plainly that the refusal
is about the characters and not about the organisation.

### And a confident wrong diagnosis, fixed

The client-parameter run printed:

```
  HTTP 403 — Access Denied — nothing written.
```

## §61 — The CHIPS money was never absent. It was never asked for.

**2026-09-12.**

`connect awards "Intel Corporation"` returned **37 awards worth $2,877,287**,
most of them NASA software licence renewals. `connect awards "Intel Federal"`
returned **9 awards worth $27,667,237**, all DoD research — IARPA AGILE/TIGRE
$9.98m, neuromorphic computing $7.4m, Stratix 10 FPGA security $4.45m, DARPA
PRONTO $3.88m, 5G Secure Slice $1.81m.

No CHIPS money appeared in either. The reader's only available conclusion was
that there is none, on a sweep whose stated purpose was Intel's New Albany
development.

### The defect

The connector filtered to `award_type_codes: ['A','B','C','D']` — contracts —
and could not be told otherwise. The footer even said so: *"Grants and loans
are excluded: different instruments, different meaning."* That sentence is
true about the filter and silent about what the filter hid.

**CHIPS Act money is financial assistance, not a contract.** It is award type
06 or 02. The query could not reach it, so the command was not reporting an
absence — **it was manufacturing one**, and labelling it with a caveat the
reader had no way to price.

This is the §42 error running the other direction. There, a transport failure
was read as a finding about the subject. Here, a query restriction was read as
a finding about the register. **Both are the same mistake: treating the shape
of the question as a property of the world.**

### The fix, and the rule it enforces

The instrument is now an argument. `--grants`, `--direct`, `--loans`, `--all`.
Each family is **its own request** — USAspending validates `fields` against
the award types asked for, so a mixed list returns 400 — and each is reported
**separately**.

**There is no row for the sum and there will not be one.**

| family | codes | what the amount means |
|---|---|---|
| contracts | A B C D | obligated-plus-potential value of a purchase |
| grants and cooperative agreements | 02 03 04 05 | money given toward a stated purpose |
| direct payments | 06 10 | payment to a named recipient — **CHIPS incentives sit here** |
| loans and loan guarantees | 07 08 | **FACE VALUE of credit**, not money handed over |

A $500m loan guarantee and a $500m contract are both "$500m" in this API and
are not the same fact. Adding them produces a number that refers to nothing.
The loan family does not even carry an `Award Amount` — it has a `Loan Value`
and a `Subsidy Cost`, the latter being **the government's own estimate of what
it expects to lose**, which is the number that belongs beside any face value
this desk ever prints.

Any run that does not ask for every family now prints **NOT ASKED FOR** with
the families it skipped, because "we didn't look" and "it isn't there" must
never render identically.

### Also fixed: raw FPDS dumps were being read as prose

Some rows carry no description. The `Description` field holds the
pipe-delimited FPDS transaction record instead:

```
200505!000720!5700!FA8104!F33615-03-D-2354!0!FA810405F0037!…
```

That was being wrapped across four lines under the heading **"What the money
bought"**, and every numeric stem in it was being fed to the word-frequency
list the reader is told describes subject matter. Those rows are now detected,
kept, labelled `NO DESCRIPTION — the field holds a raw FPDS record`, and
excluded from the word count. **Agency codes are not subject matter.**

### The command to run

```bash
bin/sentinel connect awards "Intel Corporation" --all --pages 5
bin/sentinel connect awards "Intel Federal" --all --pages 5
bin/sentinel connect awards "Intel Corporation" --direct --pages 5
```

**What a hit would establish:** that a federal instrument of a named type
exists under that recipient string, for that amount, from that agency.

**What it would not:** that the money arrived, that it paid for anything in
Licking County, or that it is connected to the New Albany parcels. §60 stands
— six of the seven questions that matter about that land deal live in county,
city and state records, and the federal register's job is to hand you the
entity names to put in the requests.

## §63 — Do data centres have a connection to bot farms?

**Not the one the phrase implies, and the real connection is better
documented.**

"Bot farm" collapses four different things that live in four different places:

| what people mean | where it physically is | is it a data centre? |
|---|---|---|
| **Click / impression fraud rings** | rented cloud and VPS capacity | **partly — rented, never owned** |
| **SIM farms, device farms** | warehouses of physical handsets | **no** |
| **Persona / sockpuppet management** | ordinary VPS + proxy networks | **no** |
| **AI content generation at scale** | hyperscale GPU capacity | **yes, and this is the new one** |

A hyperscale campus in New Albany is where ad **inventory is manufactured** and
where fraud is **detected** — not where the bots live. The industry's own
definition runs the opposite way: **traffic originating from a datacenter IP is
the marker used to CLASSIFY it as non-human**, which is why fraud operators
rent capacity in bulk and why platforms publish datacenter IP exclusion lists.

**Saying the campus is a bot farm would be an association by adjacency —
exactly the error this toolkit exists to refuse.**

### What IS connected, and is documentable

1. **The money runs the other way.** The companies building the campuses —
   Meta, Google, ByteDance — own the ad auctions that monetise the traffic.
   The campus is the **supply side of the ad machine**, and that relationship
   is disclosed in 10-Ks, not inferred.
2. **The platforms publish their own denominators.** Meta's 10-K estimates
   duplicate and false accounts as a percentage of MAU, under penalty. **That
   is a denominator a company gave you about itself** — the strongest kind
   this desk handles, and it beats any inference from a parking lot.
3. **The government buys both sides of the trade.** Already in evidence here:
   **HBGary $975,000 for ENTERPRISE BOTNET DETECTION AND MITIGATION**, and
   **Ntrepid $29,355,209** whose product line is network obfuscation and
   managed personas. Same register. Opposite functions. **Neither is secret.**
4. **The government is itself a buyer of algorithmic reach** — §64.

### The rule

**A data centre is infrastructure. Infrastructure is not intent.** The question
"who is manufacturing consensus" is answered in ad-buy records, platform
transparency filings and court dockets — **not in a building permit.**

## §73 — Fighting a lie with a better story loses. Fight it with a docket number.

**2026-09-12.** Three new subject sets, and a method note that matters more
than the sets.

### The method

Your own run this session is the argument. `courtlistener "Methbot"`,
`"Zhukov"`, `"Devumi"` — **three of four returned confident, well-formatted,
wrong answers.** The one that worked, `"click fraud"`, worked because it
named a *thing that gets litigated* instead of a conclusion.

**"Election fraud" is a conclusion. `N21C-03-257` is a document.**

A viral claim and a court finding look the same on a phone screen. They are
not the same, and the difference is not volume or confidence — it is that one
of them survived discovery, cross-examination and a judge. When you are
arguing with a Facebook post, **the only thing that ends the argument is a
citation the other person can open.** Everything else is two people trading
assertions, and the more passionate side does not win that.

So: **do not search for the truth about a topic. Search for the proceeding.**

### Thread 1 — the 2020 claims: `election_record`

The single most useful document in this entire area is a **court finding of
falsity**, not a news report:

> ***US Dominion Voting Systems v. Fox News Network***, Delaware Superior
> Court, **C.A. No. N21C-03-257**. Judge Eric M. Davis, summary judgment,
> 31 March 2023 — the court found it *"CRYSTAL clear that none of the
> Statements relating to Dominion about the 2020 election are true."*
> **Settled 18 April 2023 for $787.5 million** before the jury was sworn.

That is a judge, after discovery, with the internal communications in
evidence. It is a different species of fact from anything on either side of a
comment thread.

| proceeding | where | what it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| *US Dominion v. Fox News* | Del. Super. **N21C-03-257** | the statements were false, on summary judgment |
| *Smartmatic v. Fox* | N.Y. Sup. Ct. **Index 151136/2021** | a parallel record, still live longer |
| ***King v. Whitmer*** | E.D. Mich. — **Judge Linda Parker, sanctions opinion, 25 Aug 2021** | **the most detailed judicial autopsy of the evidence itself** |
| *US Dominion v. Powell / Giuliani / Lindell / Byrne* | D.D.C. | the claims tested as defamation |
| *In re Giuliani* | N.Y. App. Div. 1st Dep't (interim suspension 24 June 2021; disbarred 2024) | a bar tribunal's findings on the statements |

**And the denominator rule, which cuts both ways and is the honest part:**
individually prosecuted voter fraud **is real and does happen** — there are
convictions every cycle, and pretending otherwise is its own lie. A count of
them is meaningless without **the ballots cast beside it**. Anyone who gives
you a numerator without a denominator is selling something, whichever
direction they are selling it in. That is the rule this whole toolkit is built
on and it does not get suspended for a topic you feel strongly about.

### Thread 2 — the laptop: `platform_moderation`

**This is three questions told as one story, and each has a different answer.**

| the question | the register that answers it |
|---|---|
| **Are the emails genuine?** | The federal prosecution. Laptop-derived material was litigated in ***United States v. Robert Hunter Biden*** — D. | **Did platforms suppress the story?** | **Yes, and both said so.** Twitter blocked the NY Post link on 14 Oct 2020 and its CEO called it a mistake within two days. have evidence of Russian involvement."** Both halves are in one public

people quoting the half you agree with.

### Thread 3 — Minnesota: `minnesota_fraud`

**Start here, because it is the part that is not in dispute and does not need
a video:**

***United States v. Aimee Bock et al.*** and related cases, **D. Minn.** —
the **Feeding Our Future** prosecutions. Roughly **70 defendants charged**,
the largest pandemic-relief fraud prosecution in the country, federal child
nutrition money. Convictions obtained. **During the first trial in June 2024 a
juror was handed a bag containing about $120,000 to acquit** — and that
attempt was itself charged. All of it is on a public docket.

**You do not need Nick Shirley, or anyone else, to establish that Minnesota
program fraud happened at scale. The United States Attorney established it,
under oath, with indictments.** That is the strongest ground available and it
is already yours.

**Here is where to be careful, and it is the part that closes your gate.**

An indictment names **defendants, dollar amounts, programs and dates**. It
does not name a community. When a viral video takes a documented program
failure and re-frames it as a property of Somali Minnesotans, **it has left
the record** — and the moment it leaves the record it becomes attackable, and
everything true that it was carrying gets discredited along with it.

**That is why the frame matters tactically, not just ethically.** The
strongest version of this story is the one that stays inside the docket:
*these people were charged, this much money, these programs, this oversight
failed.* Pin it to a community and you have handed the other side the easiest
possible rebuttal and forfeited the documented part.

Check the oversight question in the register built for it: the **Minnesota
Office of the Legislative Auditor**, whose reports on DHS program controls are
public records, and the Minnesota agencies through `sentinel pra` — which is
the state-records half of this toolkit and has been sitting unused while we
worked federal registers.

### The one thing that actually closes the gate

When you post, post the **docket number and the date**. Not a screenshot of a
screenshot, not a headline, not a thread. `N21C-03-257`, 31 March 2023. The
person arguing with you can open it, and then it is no longer your word
against theirs — it is their word against a judge's.

**That is the whole technique. It is not more passion. It is a citation.**

**That is not an absence in the courts. It is my connector asking the wrong

### Why

`type=o` searches **published opinions**. A published opinion is what a judge
writes when there is a contested legal question worth writing about. **A
criminal case that ends in a jury verdict or a plea agreement usually produces
no opinion at all.** It produces a **docket**.

Published opinions are a small and badly unrepresentative slice of what courts
do, and this desk had been treating them as "the courts."

**This also explains the Zhukov run.** Twenty confident hits, not one of them
the Methbot prosecution — because *United States v. Zhukov*, E.D.N.Y., is a
criminal docket, and the opinion index has never heard of it. I wrote that up
as a namesake problem in §68. **It was half a namesake problem and half this.**

`--dockets` now switches to `type=r`, the RECAP archive. Opinions stay the
default because **an opinion is what you cite for a HOLDING**; a docket is what
you cite for **the fact that a case exists**. Both modes now say which question
they asked, and the opinions mode prints the other command underneath every
result — full page or empty.

### And it was wired to the wrong branch first

The flag worked when the module was called directly and **silently did nothing
through `bin/sentinel`**, because the dispatcher routes an unlisted connector
through `search` and I had only wired the direct-connector branch. The
announcement still read `type=o`. **A flag that works only when the module is
called directly is not yet a flag** — and the file already carried that exact
warning, three lines above where I failed to apply it.


The pacer spaces calls 13 seconds apart, which is **~277/hour** — it sails
straight through an hourly cap it cannot see. Two sweeps spent the budget and

## §92 — The vault accepted metadata as a document

**2026-09-12.** The bootstrap ran and put 21 documents in the vault. Three of
them are not documents.

| doc 2 | `FR 2026-00512 — CMS hearing, Minnesota Medicaid program integrity` | **1,828 bytes** |
| doc 4 | `FR 2026-03968 — CRUSH request for information` | **1,966 bytes** |
| doc 6 | `FR 2026-10817 — OMB Regulation for Federal Financial Assistance` | **21,942 bytes** |

**A four-page Federal Register notice is not 1,828 bytes. A 108-page proposed
rule is not 21,942.** Those are `fedregdoc` metadata captures — title, dates,
agency list, abstract — shelved as **PRIMARY** under titles that read like
the notice itself.

**The byte count was on screen the whole time.** It printed directly under
each title and I did not divide.

### Why it happened

`ingest_fr` globbed `*fedregtext*` **and** `*fedregdoc*` and took whichever
matched first. For the three documents where `--text` had never been run,
only metadata existed — so metadata is what it vaulted, with a full-document
title and the PRIMARY shelf.

**The shelf is the whole point of the shelf.** PRIMARY means *this is the
document*. Putting an abstract there defeats the one distinction the vault
exists to preserve — and it would have produced a citation to a line number
in a file that has no line numbers.

### Fixed

`PRIMARY` is now reserved for `fedregtext`. Metadata goes to **DERIVED**,
titled `(METADATA ONLY, not the document)`, with a note saying not to cite a
locator against it. And where no full text exists the script says so in
yellow instead of quietly vaulting the stub:

```
2026-00512 — NO FULL TEXT on disk. Metadata only is not citable.
    bin/sentinel connect fedreg 2026-00512 --text
```

Also corrected: the court-records section printed **"PRIMARY shelf"** as its
heading while ingesting **SECONDARY**. RECAP is a mirror of PACER; the court
is the primary and the mirror is a copy. **A heading that contradicts the
code beneath it is the same defect as a caveat that contradicts the table
above it (§—the vendor footer).**

And the heredoc still contained `<CLAIM_ID>` and `<DOC_ID>` — **the third
appearance of the bracket bug, inside the script written to fix it.**

### What is NOT fixed, by design

**The vault addresses bytes by hash and will not re-title what it already
holds.** Docs 2, 4 and 6 keep their wrong titles.

**That is correct behaviour and it should stay.** An audit chain that lets
you quietly repaint an entry is not an audit chain. The three mislabelled
records are now a **true record of a mistake**, sitting beside the correctly
labelled full texts that replace them.

### The three that were right

Doc 1 (`2026-00596`, **57,673 bytes**), doc 3 (`2026-05497`, 19,760) and doc
5 (`2025-19928`, 6,553) are real full texts — `--text` or `--grep` had been
run on those. **Every claim in §83 through §90 rests on one of those three.**
