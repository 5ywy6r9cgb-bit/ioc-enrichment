Delegation: **$47.4M raised · $25.2M spent · $14.6M from PACs (39.5% of all
contributions) · $39.7M cash on hand.**

**PAC% is committee money as a share of CONTRIBUTIONS** — individuals plus
committees plus party. Not of receipts, which include transfers and loans.
Eleven of these sixteen received transfers from another committee they
control; Husted's are $5.68M of his $14.35M, and computing his share against
receipts rather than contributions would have moved the number materially.

### What the spread supports

**Nine of sixteen take more than half their contributions from political
committees.** The range runs 13.9% to 74.2% — a 5.3x spread inside one state's
delegation.

**It does not sort by party.** The highest is a Democrat (74.2%) and the
lowest is a Republican (13.9%). Republican median 57.0%, Democratic median
34.2% — and the Democratic figure spans 25.6% to 74.2%, so the party medians
describe the middle and not the members.

**Jordan spent more than he raised** — $6.23M out against $4.77M in, drawing
down a balance that is still the second largest in the delegation.

**Four members hold more cash than they raised this cycle**, which means the
balance is carried forward rather than raised.

### What it does NOT support

Nothing here says a contribution changed a vote. PAC share describes where
money came from, not what it bought, and the leap from one to the other is
the entire reason this file has a status vocabulary.

## 2. A sitting senator is missing from that table, by design

**GREEN, and a limitation of the method rather than a finding.**

Ohio has two senators. The table has one. `election_year=2026` returns who is
**on the ballot**, not who holds the seat, and a senator mid-term appears in
no cycle roster at all.

This is exactly the shape of error worth naming: the table is headed
"delegation", it is internally consistent, every figure in it is correct, and
it is missing a sitting member. The tool now says so out loud when a Senate
sweep returns fewer than two.

Before any of this is published, the other Senate class has to be pulled with
its own election year and merged in.

## 3. AIPAC's $2.09m is 97% other people's money

**GREEN.** `bin/sentinel connect fecpac`, FEC Schedule B, cycle 2022.

AIPAC PAC (C00797670) disbursed **$2,089,843 across 2,482 rows**. Three
figures in the same filing say that is not the organisation's giving:

- **$248,936 to one campaign committee.** A PAC may give $5,000 per candidate
  per election. That row is fifty times the cap.
- **Average payment $842** — the size of a person's donation, not a
  committee's.
- **2,418 of 2,482 rows carry a memo code** — 97.4%.

Those are the signature of a **conduit**: individuals earmark contributions
for a named candidate and the committee forwards them. The money is theirs,
disclosed under their own names, moving through.

Reported as "AIPAC gave $2 million to candidates" it is false, and it is the
most common error made with this exact filing. The committee's own giving is
the residue — the ~64 rows without a memo code.

**Found by arithmetic failing a fact about the world.** The number was
internally consistent, correctly formatted and traceable to a real filing.
What caught it was a $5,000 statutory cap that $248,936 cannot satisfy.

## 4. AIPAC had no political committee before December 2021

**GREEN.** FEC committee registration.

| committee | type | first filed |
|---|---|---|
| C00797670 AIPAC PAC | PAC — Qualified · **Lobbyist/Registrant PAC** | **2021-12-15** |
| C00799031 United Democracy Project | Super PAC (IE-only) | **2022-01-03** |

For decades the organisation lobbied and ran no committee. Any account of
"AIPAC contributions" before the 2022 cycle is describing something else —
usually individuals identified with the organisation, which the FEC records
under the individual.

The *Lobbyist/Registrant PAC* designation is the FEC's own, tying the
committee to a registered lobbying operation. Not an inference.

## 5. UDP's spending is majority opposition

**AMBER — direction GREEN, totals need re-running.**

Schedule E for United Democracy Project returned **more spent against
candidates than for them**, from the FEC's own `support_oppose_indicator`.
That direction is a documented fact about the filings.

The dollar totals in the first run are not usable: `two_year_transaction_period`
is a Schedule A/B field, Schedule E ignores it, and the request succeeded
while returning three cycles — 692 of 1,246 rows fell outside 2024, spanning
2022-04-22 to 2026-08-11. Fixed to `min_date`/`max_date` and clamped locally;
the totals need pulling again.

Money spent **against** a candidate is not a donation to anyone, and cannot
legally be coordinated. It must never be summed with contributions.

## 6. FARA is the wrong register, and the absence is the finding

**GREEN.** FARA covers agents of a **foreign principal**. A domestic advocacy
organisation registers under the LDA and appears nowhere in FARA, so a FARA
search returns nothing — and that nothing is a fact about the register, not a
failed search.

Whether a given organisation *ought* to be a FARA registrant is a live public
argument that no database settles. Report the absence as an absence.

AIPAC's LDA footprint is complete and small: **100 filings, one client, and
the client is the registrant.** In-house lobbying, no outside firm retained
under that name. That does not rule out outside firms filing with AIPAC as
*client* — a different query.

## 7. Four institutional names, all running Lobbyist/Registrant PACs

**LEAD.**

| committee | first filed |
|---|---|
| C00479246 BlackRock PAC | 2010-03-23 |
| C00072751 State Street Bank & Trust PAC | 1976-11-15 |
| C00384818 CVS Health PAC | 2003-02-06 |
| C00160770 Walgreen Co PAC | 1982-08-23 |

Registered, dated, and connected to lobbying operations by the FEC's own
designation. Nothing pulled yet beyond the registration.

**The trap to build against before pulling holdings.** BlackRock, Vanguard
and State Street hold 5–8% of nearly every S&P 500 company because index
funds mechanically buy the index. A graph showing "BlackRock → CVS" and
"BlackRock → Vantage" looks like a network and is an artifact of indexing —
`APPEARS_UNDER` wearing a different costume.

A holding is an edge to the **holder**, never between the **held**. What
earns its keep is *change*: a position opened, doubled or exited in the
quarter before a contract award or a rezoning is a dated sequence worth a
records request. A static ownership percentage is not.
